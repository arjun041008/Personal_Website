My Research Projects in Mathematical Modelling and ML

  • Induction Head Ablation and Repetition Dynamics in Large Language Models

    alphaXiv

    This study examines how ablating induction heads — the attention mechanisms behind in-context learning — affects text generation in transformer models. It finds that removing these heads increases repetition in GPT-2 and uncovers a non-linear relationship between the number of ablated heads and the resulting repetition, pointing to the role induction heads play in preventing model failure.

  • Early Phototherapy Prediction for Neonatal Hyperbilirubinemia

    This project develops machine and deep learning models to predict the need for neonatal phototherapy using clinical and demographic data from Indian cohorts. By combining interpretability techniques with high-performing models like Random Forest and TabNet, the work identifies both established and novel biomarkers, including cord TSH, for early intervention. The system is designed as a scalable clinical decision-support tool for resource-constrained healthcare settings.

    Manuscript is currently under review.

  • Raman Spectroscopy and Machine Learning for Bacterial Detection in Milk

    Research Science Initiative (RSI India) · Indian Institute of Science (IISc)

    This project develops a rapid, interpretable pipeline for detecting S. aureus in milk using Raman spectroscopy and machine learning. Combining PCA with SHAP-based interpretability, the system achieves strong predictive performance on small datasets while remaining clinically explainable. Designed for real-world deployment, the approach offers a fast, low-cost alternative for quality control in dairy systems.

  • Interpretable Modeling of Rabies Virus Host Specificity

    This study develops machine and deep learning models to predict host species from Rabies lyssavirus glycoprotein sequences. Emphasizing interpretability, the work identifies biologically meaningful residue regions linked to host specificity while achieving high predictive accuracy. By bridging protein modeling and machine learning, the project contributes toward improved understanding of viral evolution and has potential applications in vaccine design and disease containment.

  • Fourier-Based Indicator for Financial Market Cycles

    This project introduces a novel financial indicator using the Fourier Transform to extract cyclical patterns from asset prices and predict market trends. By transforming price data into the frequency domain and filtering noise, the model isolates meaningful signals for trading decisions. Backtesting demonstrates strong performance, outperforming traditional indicators with a Sharpe ratio of 3.6, highlighting the potential of signal processing techniques in financial analysis.

  • Climate Refugees and the Future of Asylum Policy

    Published in the International Journal of Applied Public Policy

    This paper examines gaps in existing asylum frameworks for climate-displaced populations. It proposes a phased global policy model combining legal reform with quantitative migration forecasting using socio-economic and climate risk indicators. By integrating legal analysis with data-driven modeling, the work offers a structured approach to managing large-scale climate migration.
