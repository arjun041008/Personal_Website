Essays & Papers

  • The Soviet–Afghan War: Causes, Impacts and Lessons

    Academia.edu

    This research essay challenges the conventional Cold War narrative of the Soviet-Afghan War by emphasizing internal Afghan political dynamics as the primary cause of Soviet intervention. It explores the war’s military, political, and socio-economic dimensions, arguing that regional instability — rather than superpower rivalry alone — drove the conflict, with long-term consequences including the rise of extremist movements.

    Read on Academia.edu

  • The Role of the Military-Industrial Complex in the Global War on Terror

    SSRN

    This essay analyzes how the military-industrial complex shaped U.S. foreign policy during the Global War on Terror. It argues that lobbying, the “revolving door,” and defense-contracting inefficiencies incentivized prolonged conflict and inflated military spending, showing how profit motives and institutional structures contributed to both economic inefficiency and the militarization of American foreign policy.

    Read on SSRN

  • Impact of the VAT on Socio-Economic Mobility in the UK

    Zenodo

    Evaluates the UK government’s proposal to impose VAT on private school fees and its implications for socio-economic mobility. It argues that while increased public funding may reduce inequality through redistributive effects, outcomes depend heavily on efficient allocation and structural constraints, integrating economic theory with empirical evidence.

Posts & Articles Shorter pieces published on LessWrong, Medium and elsewhere.

  • Incompleteness in AI

    LessWrong

    Draws on Gödel’s incompleteness theorem to argue that AI systems face fundamental limits that leave persistent gaps for human judgment. Rather than making people obsolete, this incompleteness sustains human–AI collaboration in roles that demand non-computable, experience-grounded decision-making.

    Read on LessWrong

  • After London: The Search for the Next Global Kleptocratic Hub

    Medium

    Traces how London became the world’s money-laundering capital and, as the UK tightens its response to oligarch wealth after the invasion of Ukraine, asks which fast-growing city — from Hong Kong to Singapore to Dubai — is best placed to inherit that role.

  • The Coming African Spring: Why the Sahel May Be Poised for Revolution

    Medium

    Argues that the Sahel today mirrors the conditions that preceded the Arab Spring — a marginalized middle class, entrenched authoritarian rule, and rising digital connectivity — and that the failure of military juntas to deliver security or economic gains makes large-scale revolution increasingly likely.

    Read on Medium

  • The Arctic: The Next Frontier in U.S.–China Rivalry

    Medium

    Makes the case that the Arctic is emerging as a flashpoint in U.S.–China rivalry, as China’s interest in polar shipping routes, energy reserves, and rare-earth minerals — alongside its growing military and scientific presence — turns the region into a new arena for great-power competition.

    Read on Medium

  • Do AI Chatbots Understand?

    Medium

    Examines whether AI systems truly “understand” or merely simulate understanding. Drawing on Plato, Aristotle, and Heidegger, it argues that AI occupies an intermediate epistemic state — functionally capable but lacking experiential depth.
