  • Why do Wars become so Difficult to End?

    Eliva Press

    Wars are often easier to start than to finish. This is a product of multiple cascading internal and external factors, which shift the incentives from ending a conflict to prolonging the conflict. Afghanistan offers one of the clearest examples: over nearly five decades, foreign interventions, civil conflict, shifting alliances, and competing political objectives repeatedly transformed limited interventions into prolonged wars. This book does not attempt to provide a comprehensive account of the history of Afghanistan; instead, it focuses on drawing causal mechanisms behind prolonged conflict. Through the Soviet-Afghan War and the later American intervention, it explores misperception, political instability, military power, internationalization, and the institutional machinery of war, and how decisions made at one stage of a conflict can create the conditions for the next.

  • Incompleteness in AI

    LessWrong

    Draws on Gödel’s incompleteness theorem to argue that AI systems face fundamental limits that leave persistent gaps for human judgment. Rather than making people obsolete, this incompleteness sustains human–AI collaboration in roles that demand non-computable, experience-grounded decision-making.
